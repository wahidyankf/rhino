# Language

## Purpose

This repository is public and its consumers are not known in advance. English is the language its dependencies, its
toolchain, and its issue tracker already use, so a second language in one corner would be a barrier rather than an
accommodation.

## Standards

Write every repository-owned artifact in English: documentation, specifications, code comments, identifiers, commit
messages, pull-request bodies, and validator output.

Whenever writing in English, including replies to users, use clear, simple, natural words that readers who learned
English later can understand. Prefer the shorter word, expand an abbreviation on first use, and do not assume the
reader shares this repository's history.

Keep comments, commit messages, work notes, plans, and other documents as brief as their purpose allows. Explain why a
non-obvious choice was made, rather than only naming the choice. Preserve the behavior, steps, evidence, and other
details readers need to use, execute, or verify a document. For comments about code, follow
[Code Clarity](../development/code-clarity.md).

## Examples

In a plan, "Validate before writing so invalid inputs leave no files" gives both the step and its reason. "Validate
first" only names the step.

## Validation

Unenforced by tooling, by decision: review must judge whether the language is understandable, the reasons are useful,
and the document still contains the detail its readers need.
